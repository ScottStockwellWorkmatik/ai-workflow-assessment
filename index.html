          What this tool does

          It helps you decide which marketing workflows to improve with AI first. A workflow is a repeatable sequence of steps that turns an input into an outcome, usually across several roles. A use case is a specific application of AI to a workflow or step. You score each workflow, and the tool ranks them and suggests the use cases to start with.

          The seven questions

          How the scores work

          1. Value. The average of the two value scores, 1 to 5.
          2. Feasibility. The average of the three feasibility scores, 1 to 5.
          3. Risk. The average of the two risk scores, 1 to 5. Higher means riskier.
          4. Priority index. Value multiplied by feasibility, scaled to 100, then multiplied by a risk factor that falls from 1.0 at risk 1 to 0.6 at risk 5.
          5. Groups. Start here, Prepare first, Quick win or Park, set by whether value and feasibility are above the thresholds on the Results tab.
          6. Prohibited tier. A workflow marked prohibited is shown with a warning, is not ranked, and should not go ahead.

          The indicative EU AI Act tier

          The tier is a prompt to check, not a legal finding. It is set per workflow, so judge it by the most sensitive use of AI in that workflow. Obligations and dates under the Act have been under review, so check the current position and take legal advice. Source: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Artificial Intelligence Act, European Parliament and Council of the European Union, June 2024.

          Save, continue or compare

          1. Save your work. On the Results tab, select Download results (XLSX). You can download at any point, including part-finished.
          2. Come back later. On the Workflows tab, select Load a saved assessment (XLSX) and choose your file. Selected workflows, steps, your own workflows, scores, tiers, notes and thresholds all come back.
          3. Compare. On the Compare tab, add earlier files to see how priority has moved. Each file needs the assessment date, which the download fills in.

          Loading a file replaces what is on the page, so download first if you have unsaved work. Only files downloaded from this tool will load. The page keeps nothing between visits, so the file is your only record.

          Where the starter workflows come from

          Each starter workflow is a sequence of activities from the AI Roles Assessment, with the classification shown there (full automation, augmentation or human only). There are 31 workflows, and every one of the 31 roles appears in at least one. The grouping and order of steps are the author’s judgement and have no published source. Treat them as a starting point to challenge and adapt for your team.

          Use your own data

          1. Your roles. Load a file from the AI Roles Assessment on the Workflows tab. Workflows and steps follow the roles and activities you put in scope, including any custom roles and activities you added there.
          2. Your steps. Open any workflow and use Add a step to add an activity from the roles list, or type your own step. Added steps have a Remove link.
          3. Your workflows. Use Add your own workflow and choose its group.
          4. Your thresholds and tier. Change the value and feasibility thresholds on the Results tab, and the EU AI Act tier for each workflow on the Assessment tab.

          Workflow IDs (WF01 to WF31, your own WFC01 onwards) and activity IDs (A001 onwards, custom AC001 onwards) are kept in the Excel file. Other Workmatik tools match on these IDs, so the files can be combined later.

          About your data

          This page has no server, no account and no tracking. Your entries exist only in this browser tab and are lost when you close or refresh it, unless you download the XLSX. Loaded files are read locally and never uploaded.
